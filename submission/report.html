Sydney flat rents fell 17% in 2020, then climbed 32% above 2019. A car space now adds about $60 a week.

Once flats are matched on suburb, bedrooms, bathrooms and type, a car space added about $60 a week (8%) to asking rent in 2024 onwards, about $3,120 over 52 weeks; the simple suburb-and-bedroom gap is larger, a median $98 across 53 groups. We used past adverts to build a rent estimate for all 65,777 flats, with and without a car space. On 2,713 later adverts that the estimate never saw, the typical miss was $44 a week and 79% of estimates landed within $100 (R squared 0.888). The organisers' baseline missed by $86 with 55.2% within $100.

What we found along the way

-17% then +48%

Mix-adjusted rents bottomed at 83 (2019 = 100) in the last quarter of 2020, then rose 48% to late 2023. Three-bedroom flats fell furthest (28% from their peak). Other factors, such as supply and migration, moved at the same time, so this is timing, not proof of cause.

$60 a week for parking

Median gap in matched groups (same suburb, bedrooms, bathrooms and type) from 2024 onwards. It was close to zero before COVID, about $10 during it and $23 in 2022 to 2023. The cruder suburb-and-bedroom gap is $98 a week across 53 groups with at least 10 adverts for both parking choices (1 Jun 2025 to 31 May 2026); it is bigger because flats with parking also tend to be larger.

44% from bedrooms

Bedrooms explain 44% of the variation in rent; suburb alone explains 18%. Current median adverts run from $510 for a studio to $750 for one bedroom, $1,050 for two and $1,500 for three.

2-5% a year

Rent growth on the same flat cooled to 2-5% a year now, from 8-10% a year in 2022 and 2023. Rents are still 31-37% above their pre-COVID level.

How the momentum factor is calculated

Momentum tells the model how fast the market was moving in the months before the flat is rented.
  1. Market level. For a flat's suburb and bedroom count we take the average of the log of weekly rent over a 3-month window of earlier adverts. When few adverts are in the window, it is pulled towards the city-wide average for that bedroom count so one odd advert cannot swing it.
  2. Three-month lag. Judged adverts fall 2 to 4 months after our last advert (31 May 2026), so every level and momentum value is taken as it stood 3 months before the advert date. For an August advert, the latest window is March to May. We trained the model on the same 3-month gap, so it learns what it will face.
  3. Momentum. 3-month momentum = latest level minus the level of the window 3 months earlier (December to February). 6-month momentum = latest level minus the level of the window 6 months earlier (September to November). City-wide momentum is the same 6-month change using all adverts in all suburbs. Differences in log rent are close to percentage changes.
  4. Used with. A related factor, market growth since the flat's own last advert, helps the model move that old rent up to today's level.

What data supported the result?

106,888 earlier adverts from the supplied lease file (4 Jan 2012 to 31 May 2026) built the estimate, after removing 2,785 rows we flagged as errors (for example 10 or more bedrooms, or rents far out of line with their suburb, bedroom count and year). 2,784 later adverts tested it; 2,713 had a known parking state and were scored.

We predicted all 65,777 flats in the supplied input list (33 suburbs) and used its coordinates, bedrooms, bathrooms, parking, floor and building size. We also looked at the census block file; it did not improve accuracy so the model does not use it. No other outside data was used.

How did we estimate rent?

A gradient-boosted tree model (LightGBM) predicts the log of weekly rent, with an absolute-error loss so it targets the typical miss. It looks at the recent market rent for the flat's suburb and bedrooms and how fast it is moving, the flat's own past rents, how its building and nearby buildings compare with the market, and bedrooms, bathrooms, type, floor and parking. We predict every flat twice, with and without a car space, and never let the with-parking rent fall below the without-parking rent.

Earlier adverts→Market level, own history, building and neighbours→LightGBM→Rent with / without car space

How did we check it?

We held out every advert dated 1 Jun to 31 Jul 2026 and did not use them to build or tune the model. Model settings were chosen on April to May 2026 inside the earlier adverts. We scored three candidate models on the later adverts (a median baseline, a first LightGBM and the final one) and kept the best; the median baseline missed by $90 and the first LightGBM by $46.5. We did not retrain on the later adverts.

Typical miss by bedrooms on the later adverts: studio $30, one bed $36, two beds $56, three beds $97; with a car space $50, without $38.

What remains uncertain?

  • The judged adverts fall 2 to 4 months after our last training advert, so the real miss may be larger. A July-only check, trained through June, gave about $50 for our first model.
  • Flats with no rent history are harder (about $66 typical miss against $42 in our April to May validation), as are three-bedrooms, rents above $1,300 and waterfront or luxury areas such as Barangaroo and Millers Point.
  • The estimates ran about 0.7% above actual rents on the median in the later adverts. Rents kept moving after the training data ended, so bias could be larger in August and September.
  • The check set is 2,713 adverts; gaps of a few dollars between models are within noise. The COVID and rate-event dates are approximate.
  • We have not used floor area, building age or sale prices; those need a new dated check before claiming improvement.
