Rents fell 17%, then climbed 48%. So what should your flat rent for this week?

We built a rent estimator for all 65,777 City of Sydney flats. Four in five estimates land within $100 of the real asking rent, and we'll show you exactly how we checked.

What 112,457 past adverts told us

We went looking for the story in a decade of rent adverts. These six things stood out.

−17% then +48%

The 2020 dip wiped out 17%. The rebound added 48%.

After adjusting for suburb, bedrooms, bathrooms, parking and type, rents ended 2020 at 17% below their 2019 average. By late 2023 they had climbed 48% from that low. They now sit 32% above 2019. Three-bedroom flats fell hardest, 28% from their peak.

$60 a week

A car space went from worth nothing to about $3,100 a year.

Before COVID, flats with and without a car space asked about the same rent (the gap was around −$5 a week). From 2024 on, the gap is about $60 a week, or +8%. That is roughly $3,100 a year ($60 × 52).

2% to 5%

The market is cooling.

Compare a flat's rent with its own previous advert. In 2022 and 2023 that jump was 8% to 10% a year. Now it is 2% to 5%.

44%

Bedrooms beat postcodes.

Bedrooms alone explain 44% of rent variation. Suburb alone explains 18%. Together they explain 55%. Size matters more than twice as much as location.

Hardest

The easy flats are easy. The glamorous ones are hard.

Our biggest misses are waterfront and luxury areas like Barangaroo, Millers Point and Pyrmont, plus three-bedroom flats and flats with no rent history.

2.5%

One field in the data quietly changes meaning.

was_leased marks 3% of adverts in 2015 and 88% in 2026. It does not mean the same thing across years, so we threw it out. Roughly 2.5% of past adverts also looked like errors, and we flagged them.

What does this mean for August and September?Same-flat growth of 2% to 5% a year works out to roughly $5 to $10 a week of market movement over three months on a typical $900 rent. That is far smaller than our typical $44 miss. So for this window, getting each flat's level right matters more than guessing the trend. We have not tested how this holds further out, so we are not forecasting past September 2026.

So how good is it, honestly?

We hid 2,713 adverts from the model. Then we asked it to guess their rents. It missed by a median of $44.1 a week, placed 79.2% within $100, and explained 88.8% of the variation in rent (R² 0.888).

How we made sure we didn't cheat

A rent model that has already seen the answers will look brilliant and be useless. So we split adverts by date.

BuildBefore 1 June 2026. All training and tuning used only these adverts.
Check1 June to 31 July 2026. 2,713 adverts, scored once the method was set.
Judged1 August to 30 September 2026. 1,699 frozen adverts we have never seen.

Our rules

  • Settings were chosen using only the training period.
  • Check adverts never went into building or tuning the method.
  • Baselines and our first model were scored on the same check for comparison. We picked the final model by a rule fixed before testing.
  • Nothing dated after 31 July 2026 is used anywhere.

The messy bits, because every dataset has them

  • 24.5% of flats have no past advert at all, so we lean on market and building signals for them.
  • Bedrooms match the latest advert for 98.3% of flats, bathrooms for 99.5%, car space for 87.4%.
  • Car space is missing for 8.8% of flats and floor level for 47.7%.
  • 143 flats have unusual attributes. We kept them, because every flat needs a prediction.
  • Dropping flagged adverts moved suburb-by-bedroom medians by only 1.7%.
  • Volumes are thin before 2015, so trend work starts in 2015.

The raw data is private, so we only show aggregates.

How the estimate works

We used LightGBM to predict log rent with an absolute-error loss. That means a few outlandish adverts cannot drag the whole estimate around, which matters in a market with $5,000-a-week outliers.

MarketRent historyBuildingThe flatParking
The parking ruleEvery flat gets two predictions, with and without a car space, and rent with parking is never below rent without. The app cannot tell you parking makes a flat cheaper.
ReproducibilityFinal model: LightGBM, objective L1 on log weekly rent, learning rate 0.05, 63 leaves, minimum 40 rows per leaf, 80% feature and row sampling, about 610 boosting rounds, seed 7; recent adverts weighted more (half-life 3 years). 40 features across the five groups above. Code: https://github.com/satishpoks/microburbs-rent-task (src/02_baseline.py, src/03_model_v2.py).
How the contest judges usMedian absolute dollar error on 1,699 frozen adverts, with the share within $100 breaking ties. The scored rent comes from the column matching the advert's parking, and empty answers count worse than answered errors. That is why every flat has both predictions and no blanks.

Where we're least sure

We'd rather tell you than have you find out.

  • The real test is further out. The judged adverts fall two to four months after our last training data, so the real error could be higher. A July-only check gave $50 for our first model.
  • Our check set is modest. A few dollars' gap between models is within noise. A five-variant ensemble matched v2 on median error ($44.7) and scored a better R² (0.897). We kept v2 because we had set the rule in advance.
  • We haven't used everything yet: June and July market trends, floor area, building age and the flat sales files.
  • These are asking-rent estimates. They are not valuations or financial advice.
